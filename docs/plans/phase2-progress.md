  - **Two inspector faults found and fixed,** both in the tilt test, both making clean rendered pages Type D. Code: `raster_measures` in `src/omr/pdf/evidence.py`; spec in `docs/notes/inspector-spec.md`; 2 new tests.
    - The search tried angles from minus 3 degrees upwards and kept the first best score, so a nearly blank last page (every angle scoring zero) was measured as tilted by 3 degrees. It now tries angles nearest level first.
    - On 22 rendered pages (10 scores, MuseScore 3 and Verovio), sloping beams or sparse ink made a wrong angle score slightly better than level, by 2 to 16 percent, so they were measured as tilted by 0.6 to 3 degrees. A real tilt of 0.5 to 2 degrees scores 2 to 4 times the level score. The best angle now counts only if it scores at least 1.5 times level. The scanned commercial file and the 3 raster pages in the Phase 0 survey keep their types.

  - **Starting weights.**
    - **Detector (Engine 1): YOLO11 nano from Ultralytics (AGPL-3.0).** No detector weights trained on DeepScores with a stated licence were found.
    - **Sequence reader (Engine 2): homr, chosen by the owner,** with SMT as the fallback. homr's code and weights are AGPL-3.0. It reads one staff at a time for any instrument. Its training data includes Camera-PrIMuS, whose Hugging Face copy is listed as non-commercial, which homr's own licence notes call an open question; it is listed under "Owner to resolve" in the licence register, to settle before a release. homr's size, counted from its files: encoder 13.2 million parameters, decoder 46.9 million (stored in INT8, which is why the Phase 0 estimate of 25 million was wrong), about 60 million in all. Staff image 256 by 1280 pixels; six token streams (rhythm, pitch, lift, articulation, slur, staff position), up to 608 tokens. PyTorch checkpoints are published (checkpoint 465 matches the current training code). SMT (`smt-grandstaff`): MIT, 21.4 million parameters, bekern tokens, trained on piano grand staves only.
    - **Does homr train in 2 GB: yes, with the encoder frozen and short sequences** (owner allowed the test on 17 October 2026). `scripts/benchmark_homr_finetune.py` loads homr's own training model with checkpoint 465 (every key matched) and runs a few FP16 training steps on random data; results in `benchmark/results/homr-finetune.txt` on the corpus drive. The rendering was running on the CPU at the same time, so the speeds may be a little low. The PyTorch model has 75.0 million parameters (the ONNX files hold about 60 million; the export leaves parts out), 46.9 million of them in the decoder.
      - The whole model trained: every setting overflows the 2 GB card into shared memory. Batch 1 at 608 tokens takes 1.4 seconds a step (0.7 staves a second); batch 4 at 608 tokens 11.8 seconds.
      - Encoder frozen, decoder trained: batch 4 at up to 128 tokens fits (1.71 GB on the card, 1.6 seconds a step, about 2.5 staves a second). Batch 2 at 608 tokens just overflows (1.97 GB, 1.7 seconds a step, 1.2 staves a second). Batch 8 at 128 tokens overflows (3.0 seconds a step, 2.7 staves a second).
      - So a fine-tune of 100,000 staves for 3 passes takes about 1.4 days at batch 4 with the encoder frozen, within the plan's 1 to 2 days. Two things follow for Stage 2.1: measure how many tokens real staves need in homr's format, and split or set aside staves over 128 tokens (or train them at batch 2); and the encoder, already trained on printed music, stays as it is unless a short unfrozen run on the GPU's shared memory proves worth its time.
    - homr's source (commit ab369ac), checkpoint 465 and its training libraries (timm, x-transformers, safetensors and their dependencies, installed with `pip --target`) are in `tools/dl/homr-src` on the corpus drive, outside the project's environment.

- **Stage 2.1, training-data generator: started (17 October 2026). The label rule is written; the estimate waits for the owner's agreement before the bulk.**
  - **Label rule:** `docs/notes/raster-labels-spec.md`. It is built on a count of the names the vector reader gives on the development set (138 SMuFL names, 2.4 million symbols, 7 minutes on 3 workers). In short: 104 detector classes, grouped by what a symbol looks like, so the same mark gets the same label from every engraver (MuseScore 3's separate "m" and "f" glyphs and MuseScore 4's single "mf" glyph are both `dynamicMF`; LilyPond's clef and small 8 make one `gClef8vb` box); stems, barlines, beams, ledger lines and curves are left to the line finder (Stage 2.2); one `text` class gives Stage 2.5 its text regions; boxes are the vector reader's ink boxes, at least half a staff space a side. Labels are checked by numbers for each engraver and font, including a count of glyph-like ink with no label.
  - **Sequence-reader tokens come from the ground truth**, through homr's own parser, not from the vector reader's output. The vector reader only says which bars each staff holds, checked by counting noteheads.
  - **homr token lengths, measured** on the 300 MuseScore 4 development pairs (7,678 staves): median 22 tokens, 95th percentile 113, longest 382; 3.5 percent over 128 and 0.1 percent over 256. So nearly every staff fits the 128-token limit that the 2 GB card allows at batch 4.
  - **homr cannot write octave clefs or octave lines.** Its parser refused 68 of the 300 files for an octave clef (mostly tenor parts) and 12 for an octave line. Such staves are tokenised as they look, and the octave is restored from the clef or line Engine 1 reads.
  - **Overlap with homr's training data.** homr was trained on all OpenScore Lieder and a fixed draw of 500 PDMX files. 24 of the selected scores are among them (16 Lieder and 1 PDMX file in the development set, 4 Lieder in the regression set, 3 in the check set). Engine 2 figures will be reported with and without them.
  - **Estimate (proposed, not yet agreed):**
    - Claude sessions: 3 more (the generator and label checks, then reading the check results and fixing labels).
    - Run 1, overnight: filter the training pool and run the round trip on about 1,300 to 1,600 candidates (about 2 hours), then export 1,000 scores through the 6 jobs, 6,000 exports at about 2.3 seconds each (about 4 hours). 6 to 8 hours in all. 3 CPU workers, no GPU.
    - Run 2, overnight or a long afternoon: read the 6,000 PDFs with the vector reader (about 25 minutes), render about 35,000 pages, cut about 10,000 tiles and 100,000 staff crops, and run the label checks. 3 to 5 hours. 3 CPU workers, no GPU.
    - Disk: about 10 GB on the corpus drive (exports 2 GB, page truth files 1 GB, tiles 1.5 GB, staff crops 4 to 5 GB). The drive has 835 GB free.
    - Neither run can share the CPU with another heavy job. Stage 2.2's design and code can go on alongside.
    - 1,000 scores, not the plan's "a few thousand": 10,000 tiles need only about 700 pages, and 100,000 staves need about 4,000 pairs, so 1,000 scores in 6 jobs already give more than enough, in a third of the export time. More can be drawn later without changing those drawn.
